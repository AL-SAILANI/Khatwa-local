# STEP grammar and writing analysis: framework and gap list

Source of the framework: a third-party Arabic video course (shared by the project owner; not an official source;
received: a general STEP orientation video, grammar review parts 1 to 3, and a writing-analysis part 4). Only the
framework and routines are summarised, in our own words. Practice questions must be original.

## Orientation facts (third-party, unverified)
- Three sections: reading, grammar and writing analysis (structure), listening. An optional experimental fourth
  section (mixed) may appear and is not scored.
- Attempts: up to 10 attempts within 3 years; about 30 days of waiting before booking again.
- Rough score bands by CEFR level (course estimate, not official): A1 under 40, A2 40-55, B1 55-70, B2 70-85,
  C1 85+, C2 90+. Could inform the predicted-score model later; keep it labelled as an estimate.
- Practice sources: official sample tests, then timed mocks; train skimming and scanning for reading.
- About 29 grammar rules cover most questions; the main skill is detecting which rule a question tests by looking
  at the stem and at how the options differ.

## Core method: detect the rule first
1. Read the sentence with the blank, then compare the options: what differs (form, tense, word class, order)?
2. Name the rule from that difference, then apply its routine below.
3. Eliminate by rule, not by "sounds right".

## Rule routines (grammar parts 1 to 3)
| Area | Routine |
|---|---|
| Articles | a/an for first mention or one of many; the for known, unique, superlative, ordinal; none for plural or uncountable in general. |
| Quantifiers | Check countable vs uncountable first: many/few/a few vs much/little/a little; some/any/a lot of fit both. |
| Prepositions of time and place | at (point, exact time), on (days, dates, surfaces), in (months, years, enclosed areas); since + point, for + duration, by + deadline, in + period ahead. |
| Tenses (12) | Find time words and connectors (already, yet, since, for, ago, yesterday, by the time, while, when, next year); match the tense to them; two clauses must stay logically consistent. |
| Questions | Yes/no questions: auxiliary before subject. Wh- questions: wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb. Reported questions return to statement order with no auxiliary do. |
| Passive | be + past participle in the tense of the active verb; object of the active sentence becomes the subject; look for "by" or an action done to the subject. |
| Conditionals | Zero, first, second, third and mixed; in the unreal present use were for all subjects; match the if-clause tense with the result clause. |
| Gerund vs infinitive | Decide from the word before the blank (enjoy/avoid/finish + -ing; want/decide/hope + to; preposition + -ing). |
| Modals | Choose by function: ability, permission, obligation, advice, deduction, past regret (should have + p.p.). |
| Relative pronouns | Decide from the first words after the blank: a verb follows then who/which/that; a subject follows then whom/which/that or none; ownership then whose; place then where. |
| Question vs reported question | Direct: auxiliary first. Embedded or reported: normal statement order. |

## Writing analysis (part 4)
- Capitalization: first word of a sentence, the pronoun I, names, days, months, nationalities, languages, titles
  before names; exceptions: seasons, directions as plain words, and common nouns.
- Punctuation: full stop, question mark, comma in lists and after introductory words, apostrophe for possession and
  contractions, no comma between subject and verb.
- Word order: article/determiner, adjective order, adverb position, subject-verb-object.
- Sentence ordering: use cue words (first, then, however, finally, this, such) but logic first: topic sentence,
  support, conclusion; pronouns need their noun before them.

# Question-solving series (parts 1 to 5): what it adds

Source: five more videos from the same third-party course, solving roughly 1000 recalled exam questions (shared by
the project owner; not official). Notes below are in our own words. Coverage: the first ~2000 lines of each
transcript were read closely; the tails were only skimmed, so small rules may still be missing.

## The solving routine the series repeats
1. Read the options first, not the question. What the options are tells the rule: all prepositions, all modals,
   all quantifiers, all pronouns, all verb forms, all word forms of one root, all conjunctions, and so on.
2. Only then read the sentence and test each option by logic, eliminating wrong forms one at a time.
3. When all options are the same verb form, drop the form check and use the time line: find the time clue and
   which action finished first.
4. Many questions are decided by a single word near the blank (a time word, `of`, `than`, `was`, a name).

## Rule routines added or sharpened
| Area | Routine |
|---|---|
| Conjunctions by logic | because = reason; so = result (the clause with the result follows); but/however = unexpected or opposite outcome; and/or = listing; when/while = two events at the same time; for example = an example follows; in addition = new point; in contrast = opposite. Find the cause and the result in the sentence first. |
| Quantifiers | Decide countable vs uncountable from the noun (money, coffee, advice are uncountable). many/few/a few + countable; much/little/a little + uncountable. `a lot` needs `of`; if `of` follows the blank, pick the a-lot/a-large-number form. some = exists, amount unspecified; any = mostly negatives and questions. both takes plural verb (were); neither takes singular verb (was). |
| Capitalization | Compare option by option. Capital only for sentence start, quote start, I, proper names (every word of a multi-word name, titles before names, faculties, months). School subjects are lowercase unless the name of a course or book. A word being first in a list or sounding important does not earn a capital. |
| Relative pronouns | Person before the blank: who (subject) or whom (object) or whose (possession). Thing: which/that. Check the first words after the blank: a verb means who; subject then verb means whom; a noun owned by the antecedent means whose. which is not for people. |
| Tense from connectors | while: continuous (past continuous in the past). before/after/when with a past clause: past simple (past perfect for the earlier one). until/when/by the time in a future sentence: present simple in the time clause, will in the main clause. by the time + past simple: the other clause is past. Time words last year, ago, yesterday = past. usually, every = present simple. |
| Past perfect vs past simple | Past perfect only when one past action clearly finished before another; a single past action is past simple. |
| Present perfect | Life experience or count so far (how many countries have you visited so far), just, already; since + start point, for + length of time up to now. Questions: auxiliary first. |
| since / for / until / by | since a starting date, for a length, until up to a time. Parts of the day: in the morning/afternoon/evening, at night, by day. by + reflexive means alone (by myself). |
| used to vs be used to | used to + base verb: a past habit that stopped. be used to + -ing or noun: accustomed. Decide by whether the habit is past and finished. |
| Passive recognition | The subject receives the action (the box is made, the laptop is repaired). Verb is the third form. Choose the tense from the time clue (three years ago: was written; being repaired now: is being repaired; promise or prediction: will be delivered). Passive word order: receiver, verb, agent with by. |
| Conditionals and wish | Table: zero present/present; first present/will; second past simple/would + base, with were for all subjects (if I were you); third past perfect/would have + third form. wish about now: past simple, were for all subjects (I wish it were Saturday). |
| Gerund vs infinitive | Adjective before the blank: infinitive (easy to spend). ask, promise, decide: to + base. A time word or preposition directly before the blank: -ing. After would like, want: to. |
| Pronoun families | possessive adjective (my) needs a noun after it; possessive pronoun (mine, yours) replaces the noun; reflexive returns to the subject (yourself singular, yourselves plural, himself); each other for mutual action; subject vs object form by the role (the word after the blank decides). |
| Word forms of one root | Adverb describes a verb (worked intensely); adjective describes a noun; suffix -ity/-ness/-ion marks a noun; -ly marks an adverb. |
| Comparison | than = comparative (-er or more); the + superlative (-est or most) for one in a group; adverbs like quickly are not comparatives. |
| Possessive 's | Singular noun: 's (the kid's toy). Regular plural: apostrophe only (the cats' food). parent vs parents (one or two). |
| Titles and punctuation | Mr., Mrs., Ms. take a period. After all, with the meaning "despite everything" takes a comma; with the meaning "after all of them" it takes none. A wh-word followed by a verb is a question (question mark); followed by a noun or subject it is a statement. |
| Question formation | do/does/did + base verb; never "not" before the verb; a wh-word then auxiliary then subject. |
| Word order | Subject, auxiliary, frequency adverb, main verb, object. For paragraph ordering: introduce the person by name first, then pronouns; passive order receiver, verb, agent; the closing sentence uses finally/in the end; logic beats cue words. |
| Articles | A noun the speaker has already identified (the cake I left on the table) takes the; first mention takes a. |

## Gap analysis added by this series
Existing skills already cover most of it (gr-conjunctions, gr-quantifiers, gr-relative-clauses, gr-passive,
gr-conditionals, gr-wish-subjunctive, gr-gerund-infinitive, gr-pronouns, gr-adj-adv, gr-comparison, gr-articles,
wa-capitalization, wa-punctuation, wa-sequencing, wa-sentence-structure). Gaps:
- A time-clause skill: tense choice from while/before/after/when/until/by the time in past and future.
- A habit skill: used to vs be used to vs would.
- A word-forms skill: suffixes and part of speech from the sentence role (feeds wa-word-choice).
- Pronoun subtleties: possessive pronouns vs adjectives, each other, singular/plural reflexives.
- "Read the options first" as an explicit drill: show only the options and ask which rule is tested, before the
  learner sees the sentence. A good fit for the existing gr-strategy skill and the daily session engine.
- Tag every question by rule so the mock-exam report can show the weakest rules (already proposed above).

# Question-solving series (parts 6 to 9): what it adds

Source: four more videos from the same third-party course (shared by the project owner; not official). Notes are in
our own words. These four were read in full. The series keeps repeating the "read the options first" routine
above; only new or sharpened rules are listed.

## New or sharpened rules
| Area | Routine |
|---|---|
| Verbs that cannot be passive | Verbs with no object (arrive, happen, go, come) never take a passive form; I arrived at the hospital has no passive. A verb with a real object (deliver the package) can. |
| still / not yet | Something expected but not happened up to now: present perfect (has not arrived yet), not present simple. Present simple is for habits, routines, opinions and facts. |
| Who vs whom | The word right after the blank decides: a verb follows, the person is the doer, use who; a subject then verb follows, the person receives the action, use whom (the manager whom we interviewed). After a preposition: whom (deal with whom). |
| Question word as subject | Who / what + verb with no auxiliary: who took my pen, what happened to him. |
| Adverb of manner in comparisons | Comparing how an action is done needs an adverb (as carefully as); comparing people or things needs an adjective (as careful as). Decide by what is being described. |
| a / an | By sound, not spelling: an before a vowel sound (an official), a before a consonant sound. A singular countable job or role name needs an article (got a job as an official). |
| Purpose | Purpose of an action: to + base verb (or in order to). so that is followed by a subject and a clause, never by to. |
| Quantifier details | quite a few, a small number: countable. quite a bit, a small amount: uncountable. plenty, a lot, a large amount need of. neither goes with nor; both with a plural verb. few = not many, some = unspecified number (use some when no amount is stressed). |
| Possessive answers in dialogue | The reply mirrors the question: Is this your car? answers No, my car is over there, or It is mine. Possessive adjective needs a noun after; possessive pronoun (mine, yours) ends the phrase. |
| enough | After an adjective: old enough, rich enough. Before a noun: enough money. There is no form like to getting; use enough + to + base. |
| Gerund or infinitive after a verb | enjoy, finish, avoid + -ing; want, decide, promise, wish, need + to; make, let, help + base; like and love allow both. |
| Subject-verb agreement of a single task | A gerund phrase or group action counts as one thing and takes a singular verb (collecting insects and worms was the hardest part). |
| Reflexive pronouns | Match the subject: plural subject (Tom and Adam) means themselves; singular you means yourself; plural you means yourselves; enjoy takes a reflexive. There is no theirselves. |
| Modals for guesses | Present guess in progress: may/might + be + -ing (he may be having lunch now). General or future possibility: may/might + base. Past unrealized possibility: could have + third form (he could have gone, but he did not), joined by but, not so. |
| Conditional table (revisited) | Same four-case table as before; check the if-clause first, then confirm the result clause. Third case: past perfect with would have + third form (also negatives: would not have missed). Second: past simple with would + base. |
| Tag questions | Positive statement then negative tag; negative statement then positive tag; the tag repeats the auxiliary and tense of the statement (Mr. Harris has no brothers or sisters, does he?). |
| Time sequence for past perfect | When one past action finished before another, the earlier one is past perfect (she had made the mistake before she noticed). |
| Present perfect continuous and future continuous | Action started in the past and still going: has been studying since 6 a.m. Action in progress at a stated future moment: will be relaxing at this time next week. |
| Passive time choice | Present simple: is made. Present continuous: is being repaired. Present perfect: has been eaten. Past simple with a past date: was founded in 2005. The agent follows by. |
| Punctuation | A comma goes after an introductory clause that has its own subject and verb (After school ended, we travelled to London). Do not put a question mark on a sentence built as a statement. |
| Question vs statement word order | After how/what/where inside a statement, use statement order (I have no idea how they manage), not question order. |
| Frequency adverb and time placement | Subject, frequency adverb, main verb, object, then manner, place, time (I always drink Pepsi late at night). |
| Responding to a suggestion | A suggestion is answered by agreement or refusal, not by thanks or a repeated offer (Sounds good to me). Match the reply to what was just said. |
| Paragraph ordering | Start with the sentence that names the person or topic, not a pronoun; keep the story's chronological order. |

## Gaps this adds
Existing skills cover most of these. New gaps to add or fold in:
- A verb-patterns skill: verbs that cannot be passive, enough, purpose structures (extends gr-passive and gr-gerund-infinitive).
- Tag questions (not in the skill list).
- Modal perfect forms (could have, should have) with unrealized past (extends gr-modals).
- Dialogue-reply questions (what is the best response), which the listening and structure sections both use.
- The "wh-word inside a statement" order rule (noun clauses; extends gr-noun-clauses).
